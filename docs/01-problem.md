# 01 · Problem

Family caregiving is a years-long, medically demanding coordination job, run from a group chat and a box of paper. The person holding it together has no memory aid, and no way to share the load without sharing everything with everyone.

## Evidence

| Claim | Number | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Caregiving is mainstream | 63M US caregivers, about 1 in 4 adults, up ~50% since 2015 | AARP / NAC 2025 |
| Medical work without training | 55% do medical or nursing tasks; 22% were trained | AARP / NAC 2025 |
| Coordination is the pain | 1 in 3 find coordinating care hard | AARP / NAC 2025 |
| Singapore is super-aged | 1 in 5 residents aged 65+ in 2026; family eldercare hours projected to rise from 29 to 41 a week | LKYSPP; NBER w31958 |
| Work is disrupted | 55% of working caregivers left work for a doctor's visit in the past six months | Duke-NUS |

US and Singapore figures describe different populations. Links: [sources.md](sources.md).

## Today's options

| Option | Examples | Gap |
|---|---|---|
| Care coordination apps | Caring Village, ianacare, CaringBridge | A separate cloud app; no source-grounded memory or per-field sharing |
| Human services | Hera, Cariloop, Wellthy, Homage | Staff-heavy, sold to employers, or supply care rather than coordinate it |
| Devices for the elder | ElliQ | For the parent, not the family |
| Default | WhatsApp, notes, ChatGPT | No boundaries, no memory |

## What Kin adds

1. It lives in the group chat the family already uses.
2. It turns paper into confirmed records and reminders, with the caregiver confirming first.
3. It remembers across years, grounded in the source documents.
4. It gives each person their own view, with audited sharing on request.

Role permissions exist elsewhere (Caring Village); Kin's claim is the combination of chat, memory and sharing.
